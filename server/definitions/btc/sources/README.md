# BTC source definitions

Forty-nine logical normalized-stream contracts, plus one repair-row contract, in fifty V1 files (ruling E62, 2026-09-04, deleted the three Binance USD-M `market`-connection contracts). "Defined" is not "adopted": R1 is what promotes one — a contract is adopted when its wire shape has been observed in a capture and the adapter finalized against those frames, not when the file lands. Every file carries `status: approved` — built, tested and ready to run, the one status this program uses (ruling E45, superseding E10's single-`draft` model); whether a contract's wire shape has been observed in a capture or still rests on documentation alone is stated per contract in its description, not in the status field. Four Binance USD-M contracts were finalized on 2026-09-03 against captured hours and say so in their descriptions (below). Each JSON file fixes one homogeneous adapter-output shape:

- five Coinbase Exchange contracts: BTC-USD product status, trades, ticker and level-two book, plus the USDT-USD reference ticker (ruling E47) carried on the same socket and routed by product id — a USDT/USD reference source (like Kraken's), carrying no `quote_currency`, observed 2026-09-04 against the R1 coinbase-core capture (coinbase-core-r2-20260904T010142Z.jsonl, ~65 min): 2,612 frames replay with zero adapter errors at ~0.67/s, inter-frame gaps p50 161 ms / p99 14.2 s / max 30.6 s, quotes 0.99993-1.00014 and none crossed (stated in its description);
- twelve Kraken Spot contracts: venue status; BTC/USD and USDT/USD instruments, trades, ticker, one-minute OHLC, and level-two books; BTC/USD level-three orders;
- five Polymarket contracts: market metadata, book levels, quotes, trades, and the keyless Chainlink BTC/USD 60-second TWAP relay; and
- four Kalshi market contracts: market metadata, book levels, quotes, and trades; and
- three Binance spot contracts: aggregate trades, top-20 partial book levels, and the displayed best quote; and
- five Binance USD-M perpetual contracts: top-20 partial book levels, the five-second open-interest snapshot, its five-minute history series, the venue-declared funding interval, and the premium-index (E50) — ruling E62 deleted the `market`-connection mark-price, aggregate-trades and forced-order contracts; and
- nine Bybit contracts (group A4), **rights-gated** behind `SIGNALS_ENABLED_VENUES`: spot public trades, level-two (`orderbook.200`) book and BBO (`orderbook.1`); linear perpetual all-liquidations, folded ticker (mark/index/funding/open interest), `orderbook.50` book and public trades; and two linear REST poll identities, open interest and funding history; and
- one Pyth Network Hermes contract (group A2), **refuse-closed** behind `PYTH_API_KEY`: the BTC/USD aggregate price streamed over SSE (`/v2/updates/price/stream`) with a REST `/v2/updates/price/latest` anchor, transport `sse` (ADR 0026), the second independent source for `btc.pyth.oracle_reference`; and
- five Kalshi index-channel contracts: the CF Benchmarks BRTI 1 Hz value with its trailing 60-second average, the quarter-hour final-minute settlement window, the 5 Hz value, the Pyth BTC/USD relay, and — **repair-only** — the CF Benchmarks REST passthrough history.

`kalshi.cfbenchmarks.brti.history` is the one file here that is not a published stream. It is the versioned field contract of the repair path that closes a settlement-window pause: it emits no `SourceEvent`, carries no capture stream, and appears in no package closure or delivery catalogue (ruling R2, OD-7). Stream sources are therefore forty-nine, one fewer than the file count; the nine Bybit and eight Binance rows among them are published but rights-gated (R7), not cleared feeds.

The eight Binance contracts were published on 2026-09-03 (group A3, plus `premium-index` under E50), after ruling E62 (2026-09-04) deleted the three USD-M `market`-connection contracts. A published contract here is **not** a published feed. Binance is rights-gated: `knowledge/RIGHTS-LEDGER.md` defers commercial redistribution, and what holds the venue off the wire is the `SIGNALS_ENABLED_VENUES` allow-list, not a missing file (ruling R7) — the USD-M capture path refuses to dial unless `binance` is listed, before a byte reaches disk. The three `binance.spot.*` files promote what `crates/btc-feeds/src/binance.rs` already emits and are checked against it field-for-field on every replay run; `book-ticker` and the book's `image_kind` continuity discriminator are new adapter work landed alongside them. The `binance.usdm.*` files split on 2026-09-03, when the wave-A captures landed. **Four were finalized against observed hours (status `approved` — built, tested, ready to run — ruling E45; their descriptions state they are observed):** `book-levels` against 36,921 depth frames from a US host (the only one where `fstream.binance.com` completes a connection), and `open-interest`, `open-interest-hist` and `funding-info` against 754 polls from the Tokyo box (the only one where `fapi.binance.com` answers rather than returning 451) — two provenance lines, because no single host serves both surfaces (ruling E27). Every emitted event conforms to its file and both replays are byte-identical. Three wire facts contradicted the drafted tables and were corrected: depth carries undocumented `ps` and `st` fields, depth `pu` is **signed** (`-1` is the venue's no-predecessor sentinel on a connection's first frame), and `fundingInfo` carries a nullable `updateTime`. Probe P5 is answered — BTCUSDT **is** in `fundingInfo`, at an 8-hour interval with ±0.00300 caps.

**Three were deleted by ruling E62 (2026-09-04):** `mark-price`, `agg-trades` and `liquidations` rode the `market` connection, which is unobservable from either available host (ruling E30) — not one frame of them was ever seen, and no host we own could dial the connection. Rather than carry them indefinitely as documented-unobserved refuse-closed contracts, E62 removed all three source files, the `binance-usdm-market` capture stream, and the two signal identities they alone fed (`btc.binance.perp_cvd`, `btc.binance.liquidation_pressure`); Bybit's twins are untouched. The mark/index/funding state those families would have read now comes from the REST `premium-index` poll alone (F2/E50), so `mark_index_basis_state`, `funding_state` and `open_interest_delta` remain reachable from Tokyo and publish an honest `binance_recovery_required` until the first premium-index mark row arrives. The observed spot, `public` depth and REST siblings run.

`pyth.hermes.btc-usd.price` (group A2) is **refuse-closed behind the key but now observed**. Both Hermes update endpoints require `Authorization: Bearer $PYTH_API_KEY` and answered 401 without it (verified 2026-09-03); an authenticated hour was then captured on the Tokyo box on 2026-09-04 (`pyth-hermes-stream-r1-20260904T012850Z.jsonl`, 3660 stream frames; `pyth-hermes-rest-r1-20260904T012850Z.jsonl`, 390 REST anchors) and replayed cleanly through the adapter, so the parsed field set (confidence, exponent, EMA, slot, proof-available time) and the presence of `prev_publish_time` under `metadata` (present in every frame, chaining to the prior aggregate) are confirmed against the capture rather than documentation (`deny_unknown_fields` at every level would have failed a wrong shape loudly, and did not). Gap detection via `prev_publish_time` is confirmed (239 real chain-break gaps over the captured hour), so the staleness-only fallback is not needed. The key is env-only (`PYTH_API_KEY`), held in zeroizing memory, attached only as a request header, and never in argv, logs, notices, captures or records; the capture path exits 78 without it. `update_kind` distinguishes the SSE `stream` rows from the REST `latest` anchor rows so rolling windows exclude the latter. Adoption additionally needs the Pyth `RIGHTS-LEDGER` row cleared (R7): Pyth's terms forbid extraction, and a paid key is not a redistribution licence.

The four adopted index contracts are subscribed on Kalshi's **market socket**, `wss://external-api-ws.kalshi.com/trade-api/ws/v2`, under the market command envelope — observed on the Tokyo host 2026-09-03 (ruling E23); the per-channel paths Kalshi documents do not connect. `kalshi.cfbenchmarks.brti.value` is observed frame-for-frame, and so is `kalshi.cfbenchmarks.brti.value-5hz` — 2,202 frames on an exact 200 ms grid in the 65-minute R1 hour (ruling E25). The 5 Hz channel is **intermittently available**: the same credentials were refused with `error` code 8 on three of that hour's four connections and accepted on the fourth, so a refusal is one notice, an `unavailable` 5 Hz surface, and a retry on the next reconnect — never a lane failure, and never a change to the other channels. `…brti.settlement-window` rides an observed frame but its own conditional quarter object was never present in one, and `kalshi.pyth.btc-usd.value` has never delivered a frame at all: that surface is refuse-closed, parsed and ordered but publishing nothing, until one is captured. Each channel family is subscribed only when `SIGNALS_ENABLED_VENUES` lists its own token — `cfbrti` for the CF Benchmarks channel, `pyth` for the relay — and `btc-feeds capture --stream kalshi-index` refuses outright when neither is listed.

Pre-production revisions are consolidated in place: there is exactly one `1.0.0` contract per source identity and no deprecated duplicate. Kraken trade V1 distinguishes subscription snapshots from live updates so rolling signals cannot count historical snapshot rows as current flow.

Short captures and deterministic replay validate the active contracts at the local prototype layer; raw captures stay outside the repository. New contracts must be based on observed payloads.
